The life stages of a simulation

Every world has a beginning, middle and end.

A simulation is born, grows, surprises its makers, and eventually stops. Phases walks through each stage of that life — what happens, what matters, and what can go wrong.

Explore the phases

The six phases

Click a phase to see what happens inside it.

  1. Before a single line runs, someone asks a question: what if? This phase defines the purpose of the simulation, the rules of its world, and which parts of reality are worth modeling — and which can be left out.

    • Define the question the simulation must answer
    • Choose the level of detail (atoms, agents, or whole systems)
    • Write down assumptions so they can be challenged later

  2. The world is seeded. Starting conditions, random seeds, parameters and initial populations are set. Small choices here can ripple into very different futures.

    • Load starting state and parameters
    • Fix a random seed for reproducibility
    • Validate that the initial world is consistent

  3. Time begins to move. Tick by tick, the rules are applied and the world evolves. This is the longest phase — the simulation's working life.

    • Advance the clock in fixed or adaptive steps
    • Apply rules, physics and agent decisions
    • Watch performance and numerical stability

  4. Patterns appear that nobody programmed directly — flocks, traffic jams, markets, ecosystems. The simulation starts to tell its makers something new about the world it imitates.

    • Spot behavior that arises from simple rules
    • Separate genuine insight from artifacts and bugs
    • Decide whether to intervene or keep observing

  5. Data is collected, measured and compared against reality. Did the simulation answer the question it was born to answer?

    • Analyze outputs and visualize results
    • Validate against real-world data
    • Run variations to test how sensitive results are

  6. Every simulation ends — by reaching its goal, running out of time, or collapsing. Its state is saved, its lessons are written down, and its ending often becomes the seed of the next one.

    • Stop cleanly and archive final state
    • Document findings and known limitations
    • Feed lessons into the next conception
